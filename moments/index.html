Moments That Matter
Five minutes, prepared on purpose

One short conversation with one student, thought through before it happens. Set the context, pick a variant for each beat — why you're reaching out, how you open, the two or three questions you ask, what you listen for, what you offer, how it ends — and a prep sheet assembles itself on the right. Print it, download it as a Word file, or save your selections to finish later.

●
Caring Campus at MCC · Faculty commitment

Moments That Matter is one of the six Caring Campus commitments in the Faculty Studio. It is the highest-value and easiest-to-skip one: a brief, specific, individual check-in with a student. This tool is prep for that conversation — it does not replace it, and the conversation is the part that counts.

Stays in this browser, and asks for no student details. There is deliberately no field for a student's name — this is a prep sheet about a conversation, not a record about a person. Nothing you type is uploaded or saved to a server.

Never a score, never a prediction. Nothing here assesses a student, rates their risk, or forecasts whether they will pass. Bracketed text needs your confirmation — verify current office names and contacts before you refer anyone, and put every line in your own words before you use it.

▶ Teaching notes — running this in a session

Where it goes

This is the third of the Faculty Studio builders, after Comprehensive Syllabi and Welcome Students. Where those two are about what a whole class receives, this one is about one student and five minutes. Fifteen to twenty minutes is enough for faculty to leave with a prep sheet for a conversation they have actually been putting off.

Have them pick a real student — and never name them

The tool works best when the person filling it in has someone specific in mind. It works badly the moment that person's name gets said in the room. Set that rule out loud at the start: think of one student, describe nothing about them, fill in only the course and the setting. The absence of a name field is deliberate and worth pointing at.

The "nothing is wrong" option is the one to push

Everyone picks a trigger. Almost nobody picks the student who is quietly doing fine, which is precisely the check-in that never happens and the one with the best return. Ask half the room to build that version instead and compare the two prep sheets.

The guardrails are the teaching content, not fine print

Three things carry the weight: never a score or a prediction, refer rather than handle, and the note-to-self clause about what you write down. A private note sits outside FERPA only while it stays in your sole possession — pasting it into an AI tool ends that. Pair this with the Safe Hypothetical lab if there is time; it teaches the generalizing move this tool assumes.

Every category can be turned off

The toggle next to each section heading removes it from the assembled sheet — useful for someone who only wants the questions and the close.
