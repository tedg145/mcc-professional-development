Legal Boundaries
What may go in, and what comes back out

This runs in both directions. There is information that must not go into an AI tool at all, and there is the fact that what you type into one may itself become a public record. This is the only part of the site where being wrong has a regulator attached.

Working guidance, not legal advice. This page translates the rules into practical terms for everyday work. It does not replace the College's official policies, and it is not a legal opinion for your specific situation. When a real matter turns on any of this, ask [General Counsel] before you act.

Five regimes, in plain language

You do not need to know which statute applies. You need to recognise the category in front of you and stop.
F

FERPA — the Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act

Student education records

Grades, rosters, advising notes, conduct records, transcripts. The most common category on this campus and the easiest to hand over without noticing, because it is the everyday material of the job.

  • 20 U.S.C. § 1232g
  • 34 C.F.R. Part 99 — implementing rules
  • 34 C.F.R. § 99.3 — "education records" defined
G

GLBA — the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act, and the FTC Safeguards Rule

Financial aid data

Free Application for Federal Student Aid (FAFSA) data, Institutional Student Information Records (ISIRs), Social Security numbers. The amended Safeguards Rule has been enforceable since 9 June 2023 and reaches Title IV eligibility through the College's Program Participation Agreement and its Student Aid Internet Gateway (SAIG) enrollment agreement. FERPA's worst case is a letter; this one reaches the College's ability to disburse aid.

  • 15 U.S.C. §§ 6801–6809
  • 16 C.F.R. Part 314 — FTC Safeguards Rule
  • 34 C.F.R. § 668.16(c) — administrative capability
H

Health, counseling and disability documentation

HIPAA, Section 504 and the ADA

Make the category itself the stop sign rather than working out which statute governs — and note that the obvious answer is usually wrong here. Most student health and counseling records at a postsecondary institution are FERPA treatment records, which are excluded from FERPA's "education records" and also excluded from the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) definition of protected health information. Disability documentation carries its own confidentiality duties under Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act of 1973 and the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA). Whichever regime applies, the answer for an AI tool is the same one.

  • 34 C.F.R. § 99.3 — treatment records
  • 45 C.F.R. § 160.103 — HIPAA exclusion
  • 29 U.S.C. § 794 · 34 C.F.R. Part 104
  • 42 U.S.C. § 12101 et seq. · 28 C.F.R. Part 35
T

Title IX, student conduct and HR investigations

Sex discrimination, campus crime, mandatory reporting

A hard stop. No drafting, no summarizing, no AI notetakers, no "just help me organise my notes." Texas adds a duty most employees do not know they carry: Senate Bill 212 requires an employee of a postsecondary institution who learns of sexual harassment, sexual assault, dating violence or stalking involving a student to report it, with criminal penalties for failing to do so.

  • 20 U.S.C. § 1681 et seq.
  • 34 C.F.R. Part 106 — 2020 rule (see note)
  • 20 U.S.C. § 1092(f) · 34 C.F.R. § 668.46 — Clery Act
  • Tex. Educ. Code §§ 51.252–51.259 — SB 212
P

Personnel and human resources data

Employment records

Not FERPA, still sensitive. Note that student-employee records are education records, which catches people who assume the employment context changes the category. Medical information about an employee has to be kept separate from the personnel file under the ADA, and personnel records held by a Texas governmental body sit inside the Texas Public Information Act with specific exceptions rather than outside it.

  • 34 C.F.R. § 99.3 — student-employee records
  • 42 U.S.C. § 12112(d)(3)(B) — separate medical file
  • Tex. Gov't Code §§ 552.102, 552.117
Citations are given for reference and were checked on 22 September 2026. One is moving: the Department of Education's 2024 Title IX rule was vacated nationwide in January 2025, so the 2020 version of 34 C.F.R. Part 106 is the operative text. Confirm the current posture with [General Counsel] before relying on any of this in a real matter.

The line: green, yellow, red

One decision, made before you paste anything.
Green — any tool

Already public

Published catalog and marketing text. Public policy language. Generic drafting that carries no institutional data at all.

Yellow — licensed tools

Internal, not personal

Draft procedures, agendas, internal communications, planning documents, aggregate enrolment figures. Signed in with your MCC account.

Red — no AI tool

About a person

Student records, financial aid, health and disability, Title IX, conduct and HR, personnel files, Social Security numbers.

When in doubt, it is red.

Three behaviours

The line above is the principle. These are the three places people actually get caught.
01

The sign-in check

The distinction is never the brand. It is the contract — and the protection attaches to the authenticated session, not to the product name on the tab.

If you did not sign in with your MCC account, treat it as publishing to the open internet.
02

AI notetakers

Otter, Teams recap, Zoom AI Companion, Read.ai — anything that joins a meeting and starts transcribing. It is the highest-frequency accidental disclosure in higher education right now, because it attaches without anyone deciding to use it.

Never in an advising, counseling, Title IX, conduct, HR investigation or grievance meeting. No exceptions without written authorization from the Title IX Coordinator or General Counsel.
03

Texas public records

MCC is a governmental body. Prompts and outputs created in the course of official business are very likely public records. Using a personal account does not put the record out of reach — it makes it harder to produce, and adds a problem.

Write every prompt as though it could be printed in the Waco Tribune-Herald.
  • Tex. Gov't Code ch. 552
  • Tex. Loc. Gov't Code chs. 201–205
