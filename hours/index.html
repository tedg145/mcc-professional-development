Your week

▶ Teaching notes — running this in a session

Where it goes

Twenty minutes, immediately after the five patterns. People have just learned the taxonomy in the abstract; this is where they apply it to their own week, which is the only step that produces behaviour change after the room empties.

Get them started with a real one

Ask the room for a task out loud, type it in on the projector, and let them watch the annual number appear. The arithmetic is more persuasive in somebody's own numbers than in any slide. The quick-add buttons exist for people who freeze — do not let anyone sit there empty-handed.

The number that does the work

Most people are surprised by the annual total and unsurprised by the weekly one. That gap is the point of the exercise. Twenty-five minutes, four times a week, does not feel like anything. Seventy-five hours a year does.

The red flags are not a scolding

Tasks marked as touching student or staff records get flagged, and they still count toward the total — deliberately. The message is not do not automate this, it is this one has to happen inside an approved system. If people feel told off, they stop declaring it, and then you have lost the visibility that made the exercise worth running.

Make them leave with one

Have everyone print the plan, or photograph the top of it, before they leave the room. A plan that exists only in a browser tab is a plan that ends when the tab closes.

Do it again in a month

The second pass is more accurate than the first, because by then people have noticed repetition they missed. It also takes about six minutes.
