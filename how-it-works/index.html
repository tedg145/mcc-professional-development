1 / 3 · how it learns

It reads.

Not to memorize sentences — to learn which words tend to follow which.

It does not build a database of facts. It builds up patterns — which words cluster together, what phrasing sounds fluent, what a well-formed answer looks like. That's the whole payload it carries into every conversation. There is no lookup table underneath.

It predicts.

Prompt in, one word out, add it to the context, do it again.

That is the mechanism. The model has not looked up any facts. It has not verified anything. It picked words that were statistically likely to appear after your prompt — and it did that over and over. When the pattern is well-attested in the training data, the answer is right. When it isn't, the model still produces something equally fluent.

Separate dial.

Every heuristic we use to judge whether a human knows what they're talking about — confidence, fluency, specificity, appropriate jargon, well-formed citations — is exactly what the model optimizes for. Our error-detection instincts are trained on humans, and they do not transfer.

Which is why asking the model to "please be accurate" doesn't work. It changes the style of the output. It cannot install a fact-checking step that isn't there.

Accuracy comes from two places: grounding the model in real source material, and your verification. Not from politeness.

The four ideas — expanded

01

It predicts the next word.

That is the whole mechanism. The model produces the most plausible continuation of your text given everything it absorbed in training. Not retrieval. Not reasoning from a database. Prediction.

02

Fluency and accuracy are separate dials.

Only fluency is turned up. Every heuristic we use to judge whether a human knows what they're talking about — confidence, well-formed citations, appropriate jargon — is exactly what the model optimizes for. Our error-detection instincts do not transfer.

An AI-generated fake citation looks more like a real citation than most real citations do.

03

"Please be accurate" doesn't work.

Asking the model to be accurate, or to only use real sources, or to say "I don't know," changes the style of the output. It cannot install a fact-checking step that isn't there. Accuracy comes from grounding and from your verification — not from politeness.

04

Some tools now read documents or search the web.

When they do, they are pulling real text into the same prediction machine. Grounding narrows the failure mode, it doesn't remove it. The model can still fluently misread what you gave it.

A useful nuance for the sophisticated third of the room — not the starting point.

  • Hand out the QR. Bottom-left button, or Shift + Q. Encodes the current URL — including any fragment. Everyone lands on this page and can click through themselves.

  • Two things to say out loud. One: the probability numbers are illustrative — the mechanism is real, the exact distribution is not.
