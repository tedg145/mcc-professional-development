Assignment Stress Test
Let the models read your directions first.

Paste an assignment exactly as students will see it. Several AI models each try to complete it and say where they had to guess. When three models read the same directions three different ways, the directions are unclear. When all three finish it easily, a student could too.

▶ Teaching notes — running this in a session

Start with the worked example

The sample sociology essay looks ordinary. Three models read "proper formatting" as APA, MLA and ASA. That one disagreement makes the point faster than any slide: if the models guessed three ways, so will thirty students.

Run it in thirty minutes

Five minutes on the worked example together. Fifteen minutes: each person pastes one of their own assignments, runs the prompt in two tools (or LEX once), and pastes the answers back. Ten minutes: pairs pick one flagged gap and one redesign move, and rewrite one sentence of the directions together.

Disagreement is the useful signal

One model's guess is just a guess. When several models land on different readings of the same words, the words are the problem. That is why this lab asks for more than one model, and why a tool that shows several engines side by side fits it so well.

HIGH is not an accusation

Plenty of good assignments are easy for AI as written. A HIGH rating means the final product alone no longer shows you the learning, so you change what you grade, not whether the assignment is worth doing. And a LOW rating only helps if it comes from what students bring, not from directions so vague nobody could finish them.

The model's rating is an opinion too

Models are not reliable judges of their own abilities. Treat the rating as one more reader's view and look at the actual attempt it wrote. If the attempt would earn a B in your class, that is your answer.
