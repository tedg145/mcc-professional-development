▶ Teaching notes — running this in a session

Where it goes

This is the Caring Campus "Welcome Students" commitment made concrete. It pairs well with the Syllabus Builder — the syllabus sets the terms of the course, this sets the tone of the room. Twenty minutes is enough for faculty to leave with a script they'd actually use.

The point is the draft, not the wording

Nobody should read these paragraphs aloud verbatim, and the tool says so. What the options do is show that there is more than one defensible way to open a class, so a participant who has been opening with "alright, let's look at the syllabus" for fifteen years has somewhere else to start. Ask people to rewrite one chosen clause in their own words before they leave.

Match the options to the room

Each category carries tags for format and size — large section, online, staff meeting. A faculty member teaching a 120-seat lecture and one teaching a 12-seat lab should not walk out with the same script, and the tags are the fastest way to make that visible.

Support and fairness is the section worth slowing down on

Most students never ask for help because nobody told them it was available or how to ask. The Support & Situational Fairness options are about saying it out loud on day one. Before running this, confirm the current names and contacts for the offices in the brackets — a referral to an office that was renamed two years ago costs a student the trip.

Every category can be turned off

The toggle next to each section heading removes it from the assembled script entirely — useful for a short meeting, or a second-day welcome that doesn't need the full arc.
