You already wrote the hard part: the lecture outline or the reading. Paste it once and this lab builds five prompts that keep the AI tied to your material: a study guide, a practice quiz with an answer key, discussion questions, a plain-language summary, and alt text for your images. ▶ Teaching notes — running this in a session

The point is the checklist, not the prompts

Faculty will leave with five useful prompts, but the habit is the real lesson. Grounding narrows the failure mode; it does not close it. A quiz built only from your notes can still have two defensible answers, or an answer key that quotes a sentence that does not quite say what the answer claims.

Run it in twenty-five minutes

Five minutes: everyone loads the sample or pastes their own outline. Ten minutes: each person runs the quiz prompt in their tool of choice. Ten minutes: trade quizzes with a neighbor and work the checklist on each other's answer key. Ask how many found a problem. Most tables will.

Why the quiz asks for a quote

"Quote the sentence that supports the answer" turns checking from rereading your notes into matching a quote. It also exposes the common failure: the quote is real, but it supports a slightly different answer.

Alt text is the one that needs the image

The tool cannot see an image you have not attached. Without it, the tool will happily describe what it expects the image to show. That makes a good live demonstration of the fluency-versus-accuracy point.

Which tool

Any of them. Microsoft 365 Copilot is available with your MCC account. Running the same prompt in two tools and comparing the quizzes is a fast way to see that neither one is the answer key.
