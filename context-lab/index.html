What this shows

Some words narrow the possibility space. Some words reopen it.

Grammar words — articles, prepositions, conjunctions — usually open the door back up. After "to", almost any verb could plausibly follow. After "about", almost any noun. The model's uncertainty spikes right back up.

Content words — nouns, specific verbs, named entities — usually collapse the space. "Neil ___" leaves the model with a strong bet on "Armstrong". "MCC ___" leaves it groping.

This is why prompt engineering works, and it is not magic phrasing. When you write "You are an academic advisor writing to a first-year student who missed a deadline," you are not being polite to the model. You are collapsing a thousand possible response styles down to a few. Adding the words rules out everything they exclude.

This also explains why fluent-and-wrong is the model's most dangerous failure mode. When your prompt has narrowed the space enough, the top candidate can be very confident. That confidence is real — about the probability. It says nothing about whether the answer is true. Both properties are compatible with the same distribution.
