Documents & Spreadsheets Lab
Where the time savings are, and where the quiet errors are

Summarizing a report and writing a formula are two of the biggest time savers AI offers. They are also where mistakes slip through, because the result looks finished. Check a summary against its pages, then check four formulas against numbers you already know.

▶ Teaching notes — running this in a session

The report: grounding narrows the failure, it doesn't close it

Giving a tool your own document is a real improvement over asking it from memory. It still makes three kinds of mistake, and all three are here: a wrong number with a correct page, a correct fact with the wrong page, and a claim that isn't in the document at all. The follow-up question is the one to slow down on. The report says no cost estimate was prepared, and the tool produced one anyway, with a page number.

Teach the habit, not the catch

Summaries are for deciding where to read. Any number or claim someone will act on gets checked on the page. The prompt "Where in the document does it say that?" is in the Prompt Template Builder.

The spreadsheet: check on rows you already know

Three of the four formulas are wrong, and none of them errors out. The range that stops one row short and the SUMIF whose sum range is shifted by one row are the most common AI formula bugs. The average is the hardest: it is off by about two dollars. Ask the room whether "close" is good enough when the number goes in a budget request.

Run it in twenty-five minutes

Pairs. Ten minutes on the report, ten on the spreadsheet, five to debrief. Ask which mistake was hardest to see. It is almost always the number.
