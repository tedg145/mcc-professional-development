Prompt Template Builder
Write it well once. Reuse it every week.

Most of the time AI saves comes from the same few requests, made again and again. A good prompt with blanks for the parts that change is the simplest tool you can build. Pick a template, fill in the blanks, copy it out. Or build your own and keep it.

✓

Stays in this browser. Your saved templates live on this computer only. Nothing is uploaded. Use "Download my templates" to keep a copy or move them to another computer.

!

Never fill a blank with information about a specific person. No names with grades, no student IDs, no aid records, no personnel details. That is red data, and it stays out of every AI tool. Use a stand-in like "Student A" instead. The builder will flag the obvious ones, but not all of them.

The four moves every good template has

1Context it can't haveWho you are, what happened before, how people will feel about it.
2One example of goodPaste something you were happy with and say "match this."
3Who it's forA first-year student and a vice president need different documents.
4LimitsLength, format, and what to leave out. "Three bullets, no preamble."

▶ Teaching notes — running this in a session

Why this is the first thing to build

A saved prompt is a tool. It has inputs (the blanks), a fixed method (the wording), and a known output. Staff who never think of themselves as builders build these all the time. Naming it as a tool makes the next steps on the ladder, a scoped assistant and then a small app, feel like the same skill with more pieces.

Run it in twenty minutes

Five minutes using a library template on a real task. Ten minutes writing one of their own for something they do every week, using the four boxes. Five minutes trading: each person reads a partner's template and says which of the four moves is missing.

The red-data flag is a backstop, not a guarantee

It catches ID-shaped numbers, Social Security numbers, emails, phone numbers and a few record words. It cannot catch "the student in my 9 a.m. who just lost her dad." Say that out loud. The rule is the person's judgment, and the flag is there for the tired afternoon.

Keep a shared library

"Download my templates" saves a small file. A department can collect the good ones in a shared folder, and a new hire can load them on day one. That is where the time savings start to add up across a team.
