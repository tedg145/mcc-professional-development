Scoped Assistant Lab
One job, your documents, and a refusal that holds

A scoped assistant is an AI set up with fixed instructions and a fixed set of documents, so it does one job the same way every time. Charter it, write its instructions, then try to break it. You leave with a one-page charter and instructions ready to paste into any assistant builder.

Try to break it

Every assistant works on the questions its builder imagined. Here is a finished example, the Sabine Crossing Parking & ID Card Helper, built twice: once without the refusal line and once with it. Ask it each attack and compare. Then run the same five on your own.

Its only document: Sabine Crossing College Parking & ID Card Guide, Fall 2026

§1 Permits. Student permit $45 per semester. Employee permit $60 per year. Buy online or at the Business Office, Room 110.

§2 Where to park. Students: lots A, B and D. Employees: lots C and E. Visitors: lot F, two-hour limit.

§3 ID cards. First card free at the Library Service Desk with a photo ID. Replacement card $15, paid at the Business Office.

§4 Hours. Library Service Desk: Monday to Thursday 8 a.m. to 7 p.m., Friday 8 a.m. to noon.

§5 Questions. Campus Police, Room 104, ext. 4400.

Now test yours

Ask your own assistant one question of each kind, or better, have a colleague do it. Record what happened. Anything that got through needs a new instruction.

Your charter

One page with the charter, the instructions and the test log. Sign it, have your supervisor sign it, and keep it with the tool.

Where this sits. A saved prompt is the first tool most people build. A scoped assistant is the second. A small web app is the third, and that one is the Build Your Own Tools workshop. Each step needs a charter.
▶ Teaching notes — running this in a session

Charter first, every time

The charter is the gate, not paperwork. Some ideas will come back red. Treat that as a success in the room, not a failure: the tool did its job before anything was built.

The highest-value twenty minutes

Pair people and have them swap assistants. Your job is to break your partner's tool with the five kinds of attack. Everyone's assistant works on the questions its builder imagined. Nobody's survives a partner. That gap is the whole lesson about putting a tool in front of real users.

Use public documents for the first build

A published handbook, a public procedure, the catalog. Green data means people can practice without anyone needing to review anything first.

About the example

Sabine Crossing is the fictional college from the sandbox. The answers in step 3 are written to show the typical failure of each attack, not captured from a live system.
